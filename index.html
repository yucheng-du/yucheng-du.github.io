About me

I study how language models represent what they can and cannot answer, and how those internal signals shape their behavior. My work combines representation analysis and causal interventions to understand LLM reliability. I also work on AI auditing and computational linguistics.

Publications

EMNLP 2026 · Main Conference Published

Recognition–Refusal Misalignment in LLMs: Why Models Answer Structurally Unanswerable Questions

Yucheng Du, Xiyang Hu

LLMs encode when math and code questions are structurally unanswerable, yet they often answer instead of abstaining. This recognition signal is nearly orthogonal to the safety-refusal direction, and activation steering along it changes whether models acknowledge the invalidity.

Proceedings DOI 10.18653/2026.emnlp-main.1078 arXiv:2608.29109 Code & data

TrustNLP 2026 · ACL Workshop Published

Geometric Deviation as an Unsupervised Pre-Generation Reliability Signal: Probing LLM Representations for Answerability

Yucheng Du

Distance of an LLM’s hidden states from answerable reference representations provides a label-free, pre-generation signal for detecting unanswerable math prompts, without labeled failure examples or access to model outputs. The signal is weaker in code and does not reliably separate factual prompts.

Proceedings DOI 10.18653/v1/2026.trustnlp-main.22 arXiv:2605.03196 Code & data
